## Post 2: Reddit (r/ClaudeAI)

**Title:** Co-wrote a 141k-word hard sci-fi novel with Claude — here's what I learned about its prose patterns

**Text:**

I'm a professor in applied engineering. Between semesters I took my agentic coding workflows and pointed them at fiction. The result is a complete novel, free under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

The book is called *Checkpoint* — set in 2041, 900M people with brain-computer interfaces, four POV characters across four continents. Ironic subject matter for an AI-assisted novel, which was the point.

**What I noticed about Claude's writing:**

Claude's prose converges toward a recognizable statistical mean. I ran a basic computational analysis and found specific patterns:

- **Philosophical codas:** Claude loves ending scenes by explaining the thematic weight. "And that was the truth of it — the world had already moved on." I cut ~30 of these.
- **Hedging language:** 57 instances of "slightly," 53 of "almost." Claude qualifies everything.
- **Glue gestures:** Characters nod, sigh, and "exchange meaningful looks" to keep scenes flowing. Robotic connective tissue.
- **Voice convergence is measurable.** I tracked sentence length per character across the story arc. By Part IV, cross-character standard deviation dropped from 1.04 to 0.32. Four characters who should sound different started sounding the same — which mirrors the plot.

I used Gemini to audit Claude's patterns, then had Claude surgically fix its own tendencies. I also built some text analytics comparing the novel to Project Gutenberg classics (1984, Frankenstein, We) on emotional word density and sentence structure.

None of this is scientifically rigorous, obviously. No control group, no ablation, no peer review. For me it was an interesting side project to explore the probability landscape of LLMs in combination with CLI tools like Claude Code — what happens when you take agentic workflows from research and coding and just keep going until there's a novel at the other end.

Everything is at [checkpoin.de](https://checkpoin.de) — read online, PDF, audiobook, full repo.

Curious if others have hit the same convergence problem with long-form writing.
