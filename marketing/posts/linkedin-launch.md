# LinkedIn Launch Post

Between semesters, I usually finish a draft paper. This time I finished a novel.

I work with machine learning in applied engineering research — recently agentic workflows and autonomous systems. I took that same approach and pointed it at fiction. The result: a complete hard sci-fi novel, an audiobook, a website, and a social media campaign. From zero to published in days.

The novel is called *Checkpoint*. Set in 2041. A brain-computer interface — a small patch behind the ear — has been adopted by 900 million people. It reads the brain. It also writes to it. Four lives at the edges of what it means to think for yourself in the era of AI.

It was co-written with Claude (Anthropic). The process raised one of the questions the book itself asks: where does the machine stop and the human start?

One thing I can confirm: the review took far longer than the generation. Writing is fast now. Knowing what's good is still slow. That shift changes more than people think.

The whole thing is free under Creative Commons. Read it, listen to it, look under the hood:

https://checkpoin.de

Consider it a case study: what happens when you take agentic workflows out of code and science and into creative work? I'm curious what people think — about the text, the process, the implications. Comments and corrections welcome.
